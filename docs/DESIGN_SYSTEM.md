# Design system and interaction contracts

Notchy uses one black notch surface. Modules share that surface; the notch owns the outer contour, animation and native window geometry. The latest Repos, Module Musique and Grille Notchy exports refine the final step 22 designs within the existing maximum width. Ordinary routes retain their existing presentation.

The source of truth for geometry, spring, timings, accents and spacing is `ISLAND_CONFIG` at the top of [notch.js](../notch.js). [notch.css](../notch.css) owns the content layout. The JavaScript constants are exposed to CSS as custom properties.

The brand mark is Mimo's original robot head, using the same shell, eyes and mouth as [welcome.js](../welcome.js). [notchy-mark.svg](../assets/notchy-mark.svg) places the white head on a black tile for documentation, and [icon.ico](../assets/icon.ico) supplies the corresponding Windows application and tray icon in multiple resolutions. The NSIS installer uses the same file through `bundle.windows.nsis.installerIcon` in [tauri.conf.json](../src-tauri/tauri.conf.json).

The table gives the central body width. A hover activity row has a painted 8 × 8 px ear on either side, so its complete silhouette is 16 px wider. Reference 19.2 supplies the row, badges and spacing; the user's reduced corners and ears override its larger rounding. The discreet tab has no ears; the expanded panel retains its existing contour. Native hit testing follows the same curves, including the ears and excluding their transparent cutouts. Windows display scaling changes the physical screenshot size, not these CSS dimensions.

The discreet tab always stays thin, including while music, Focus or a transfer is active. The close-up Repos reference places a 70 × 2 px progress track at x=14 and y=4 inside its 128 × 10 px body. Music uses a white fill; four centred 2 px spectrum bars occupy x=96..110, leaving a 12 px separation. The spectrum uses real loopback audio and its artwork colour, with white as the fallback. Silence and pause leave the bars stable; no CSS loop simulates sound. Focus alone uses a blue fill and the reference's 4 px blue marker at x=100. With both activities present, their progress shares the same 70 px track and Music keeps the spectrum. The tab contains no title, artwork or old orange activity indicator. With no activity, it shows only the centred 28 × 2 px handle. A current transfer owns its dedicated progress indication. Completing or dismissing onboarding returns directly to the ordinary discreet tab.

Hover reveals the activity row without opening the central panel: one primary label and real value, then up to two secondary icon badges. Focus has priority, followed by Music and an active LocalSend transfer; a running file operation can use the last place when no transfer occupies it. Countdown values and transfer progress come from their real state. A file operation without measurable progress stays indeterminate. An idle LocalSend listener, discovery screen, saved files, weather or task count does not create an ongoing activity. The row uses the reference's 240/300/360 × 42 px sizes without the previous extra enlargement. The full panel opens only after a click or keyboard activation. Paused Focus keeps its actual remaining time. The export's looping activity count, fixed timer text and simulated progress are demonstration placeholders rather than application behaviour.

Compact, Standard and Large select widths of 540, 567 and 594 px. Ordinary routes, Settings, the queue, Shelf and LocalSend keep the 324 px height. Nook's scene uses 324 px for one, two or three placed modules. The final exports' wider demonstration canvases are adapted to these dimensions rather than enlarging the native window. Welcome and tutorial retain 380 × 232 px. The rounding control adjusts only the open contour around its default radius. Smaller work areas reduce the available dimensions. Extra form or list content scrolls inside the surface.

Secondary activity badges have transparent backgrounds. Music's measured spectrum and LocalSend's progress ring sit directly on the shared notch surface or cover-derived backdrop, without separate dark discs. Their geometry, progress and motion remain unchanged.

## Content grid

The shared header is 48 px: 8 px above and below its 32 px controls. Ordinary routes have 16 px side and bottom margins, leaving a 562 × 260 px content zone at the maximum size. In the explicit Essentials composition, the navigation footer occupies the final 32 px, leaving a 562 × 228 px grid. Its 4 × 4 cells have 8 px gaps: each cell is 134.5 × 51 px; two columns span 277 px and two rows span 110 px. Width follows the selected format and available work area; the shell dimensions do not follow the selected composition.

Nook and its editor share the pure `modulePlacementPlan` helper and the first three configured slots. Music, Focus, Tasks, Weather and Shelf remain available in the catalogue, including when they have no live activity. One module fills the scene. Two use equal halves. With three, the primary keeps the left half and two quarter cards stack on the right. The editor keeps all three whole destinations, including empty drop targets; its primary destination follows the first occupied slot. A fourth saved module remains enabled in reserve. The live activity band remains independent and never treats weather, saved files or an idle timer as an ongoing activity.

The header has the same 48 px height, 8 px top inset and 16 px side inset on every ordinary route, scene and editor. Its three visible entries are Nook, Tray and Settings, with their original filled icons. Weather opens from its activity card. Settings contains General, Modules, Scenarios and Health. Temporary status text is absolutely positioned and cannot push navigation sideways. The scene starts at 48 px, with 16 px side and bottom insets and an 8 px card gap. Surfaces share a 12 px corner token, rectangular controls use 8 px, and artwork uses 6 px. Circular controls, progress rings and switch thumbs retain their circular shape. The existing rounding preference maps to a bounded 12–16 px outer contour; native hit testing consumes the same canonical radius. The scene uses no footer or Reduce control.

Each catalogue module and a contextual transfer has three declared presentation families, exposed as `data-presentation`: `full` for the sole main module, `scene` for either half and `rail` for either quarter. At the maximum width a half measures 277 × 260 px and a quarter 277 × 126 px. Quarter Focus places the linked task beside a 56 px progression ring; the half uses a 108 px ring and total duration. Quarter Weather shows condition, city, temperature and actual update freshness over a condition-dependent gradient. The two-column composition remains intact at the 440 px reference width. Smaller content uses internal scroll where needed without changing stored placement. Text is never scaled with the outer spring. No presentation creates a duplicate timer, file store or media controller.

Weather follows reference 22.2. Its full card contains current conditions, the narrative forecast, six hourly values and three daily ranges. The shared main card retains the narrative and four hours; the small card retains the temperature, condition, apparent temperature and two hours. All variants use the same validated forecast and city preference. Missing or stale values stay labelled as such; the interface never fills an absent forecast with sample readings. Location, city selection and refresh remain available on the Weather route.

Tasks follows reference 22.3 and is independently placeable. Its full card has five visible list rows and a current-task column; its shared main card has four rows and an add action; its small card shows the current task and Focus action. Longer lists scroll internally in whole-row viewports. Add, selection, completion, deletion and cycle estimation use the existing Focus model and storage. A task starts an idle timer, resumes a paused timer, or changes the association of a running timer without resetting its deadline. Installing Tasks alone keeps that same timer available. Legacy Focus profiles and task identifiers remain compatible.

In hover previews, decoded artwork supplies one opaque gradient across the entire SVG silhouette, including its shoulders. The same backdrop remains behind Focus and transfer activities while the current media is available, including pause. Missing or failed artwork, the discreet tab and expanded routes retain the ordinary surface. Palette stops interpolate over 320 ms and the surface fades over 180 ms; reduced motion and disabled animations remove those transitions, and forced colours hide the artwork layer. Foreground artwork uses centred contain sizing in both the main music preview and its secondary badge, preserving portrait and landscape images without cropping. The gradient uses the existing contrast-bounded palette and never affects native hit testing.

Music follows the new Module Musique export. Its opaque, cover-derived colour surrounds artwork that fades into the card: from the left in the full card and banner, and from the top in a half. The original framing and gradient masks remain in place. Foreground artwork has a modest saturation of 1.12 and contrast of 1.02, controlled by --music-art-saturation and --music-art-contrast. The original dark veil and background palette remain unchanged; the adjustment affects only the image, keeping text and control colours intact. The secondary rail anchors artwork to the entire card rather than a text grid cell. Historical editor artwork previews share the image treatment. A mini preview keeps a complete 30 × 30 px square cover, title, artist and three white solid transport controls inside the existing hover silhouette. The 34 × 34 px frame uses a 6 px inset and 4 px corners. Its image has a 2 px internal margin, preserving a complete 30 × 30 px contain area without rounding away the source corners. Explicit image dimensions prevent portrait content from enlarging the intrinsic grid row. The rail clips its own painted content against the current lower radius, independently of native hit testing. The full and half cards retain elapsed and remaining time; a narrow banner and mini omit the time labels but keep real progression. Playback uses rounded solid glyphs, a single triangle and bar for previous/next, and a white circular play/pause button. Shuffle, repeat, queue and add-to-library use outline glyphs; the library action remains disabled when the Windows session does not expose it. The main player, hover transport and secondary activity share the same icon shapes and playback state. The small queue button opens Up next and secondary controls for shuffle, repeat and opening the detected player. Closing it returns to the prior Nook page and a visible trigger. The same media host moves between primary and secondary placements; its timeline, capabilities and source identity never reset because of layout. Only the background palette has bounded luminance to keep the artist readable; only the foreground cover receives the modest saturation/contrast adjustment, while the real audio spectrum retains the cover's accent and a white fallback. There is no glass blur or simulated audio loop. A system player that does not expose its queue shows that limitation and removes old Spotify rows. Queue polling runs only while its explicit route is visible. Long connection messages scroll internally.

Reference 22.5 links optional tasks to the existing Pomodoro. Each elapsed work phase credits one cycle to the task associated with that phase; skipping a phase does not manufacture credit. Task actions settle an elapsed deadline before changing the association. A completed cycle asks whether the referenced task is finished, without automatically checking it. Confirming or checking a linked task offers another task or free Focus while preserving the timer. Prompts persist by task ID across reloads and are invalidated when that task is deleted. The shared prompt works even with only Tasks installed. It appears in open Nook, Focus or Tasks, without opening the panel or covering Settings, editing, configuration or LocalSend consent. Keyboard responses return focus to a visible control; automatic timer events never take focus.

Focus follows reference 22.6. Its full card has three zones: horizontal rolling minutes and seconds with their progress bar and linked task; a compact task list; and transport with explicit Complete and Next task actions. The shared main card keeps the clock, task and transport; the small card retains the same horizontal clock, task, bar, Complete and playback controls. Next task changes the association without skipping a Pomodoro phase or resetting the deadline. Configuration opens the full Focus route, and the task picker stays inside the card. Digits retain their slide and fade, with a simple fade when reduced motion is enabled. Main cards and summaries use bars; the hover activity keeps its ring, and the discreet tab keeps its centred bar.

Right-click opens an opaque editor with three whole destinations, the module catalogue centred in its dock, and Apply/Cancel controls in the top heading. Draft changes, keyboard selection and drag exchanges share the same placements as Nook, including equal columns when all three destinations are filled. Apply shows the resulting Nook layout; Cancel restores the previous view and saved arrangement. Four-slot legacy storage remains compatible: the fourth entry is a reserve available from the catalogue, and moving it into a visible slot swaps it without deleting module data.

Selecting a rail card promotes it and swaps the former primary into its place. The configured first module owns the scene initially; automatic Focus updates do not override that choice. Promotions remain stable through clock ticks and progress updates, and applying a new arrangement resets the temporary promotion. A live transfer appends when space is available or temporarily replaces only the last rail; it never rewrites saved slots. Its completed card briefly confirms before the configured module returns. Focus and Tasks share one underlying timer and task list across every presentation. Music and LocalSend retain their measured progress bars.

The four footer indicators select Music, Focus, Tasks or Essentials. Music and Focus return to the configured scene; the dedicated Tasks and Essentials routes remain available:

| Composition | Cell placement |

| Music | The real player fills all four columns and four rows. The queue replaces its controls within the same area. |

The pure `nookGridPlan` helper declares one-based cell positions and spans. `createActivityScene` owns stable scene and rail assignment. Disabled modules are omitted; utility destinations do not install additional modules. Cards do not duplicate timer models, IDs or event handlers, and do not import demonstration labels as application data.

Settings follow the supplied reference: two #161618 cards, segmented format buttons, a rounding slider, a mint animation switch, four mint icon rows and a language footer. Cards use the available width, and additional controls scroll inside the panel. Shelf remains accessible through Tray; file tools and LocalSend retain their existing flows. Shelf has a fixed-height grid row inside the existing panel. Its header and selected-file actions remain visible while only the file list scrolls. This includes multiple selection, a single image with contextual tools, and a long file list. Below 460 px of available notch width, nearby sharing uses a bounded 52 px second row; it does not expand the shelf or displace its actions. The file area stays in the first row during an incoming transfer, overriding the older viewport-based row permutation. Nearby devices remain reachable by scrolling their bounded area.

Scenario preferences, task data, timer deadlines and transfers remain intact when changing pages. Focus selects the Focus composition, Ambience selects Music, Day and Complete select Essentials, and Minimal selects the first available primary view. A saved choice is restored at launch. An automatic change can be undone to the previous visible composition, including a prior manual page. Changing a composition never opens a closed island or introduces a wider panel.

## Motion contract

Width, height and lower radius share an analytic spring with stiffness 400, damping 30 and mass 1. It settles within 500 ms, with approximately 2.8% overshoot. Retargeting preserves the current position and velocity. Content keeps its final width throughout that motion.

Expanded content enters after 80 ms: opacity and blur over 300 ms, translation from 20 px and scale from 0.95 over 500 ms. Its 150 ms exit finishes before the surface closes. Compact content uses 5 px and scale 0.9. Leaving layers are inert. The header belongs to the same fixed content layer, so its controls do not drift with the contour.

All music spectra consume measured native audio bands, including the thin tab, hover row, main player and secondary activity card. Bar transforms interpolate over 80 ms; silence stays at its quiet baseline rather than starting a decorative loop. Artwork extraction supplies every spectrum colour, with white when no usable cover exists. Reduced motion stops the capture subscription and spatial changes. The native loopback source is the default Windows output mix, so other desktop audio can be included while music is playing. Transfer rings use real progress or an indeterminate spinner when no measured percentage exists. Music, Focus and LocalSend progression share the linear progress duration in tokens.css; their values still come from real clocks or measured transfers. Navigation indicators grow from 6 to 18 px over 400 ms. Activity and status colours now follow [the shared tokens](tokens.md): Focus work stays mint, short breaks blue, long breaks purple and LocalSend transfers a distinct pale blue. Success, warning and error keep separate status colours; music follows the real artwork palette.

Pointer detection follows only the visible black contour in every state. Native cursor polling tests the actual DPI-aware Windows region; transparent margins, ear and rounded corner cutouts, and the area below the visible surface are outside. There is no enlarged invisible target. A 120 ms dwell previews the collapsed activity details; a quick pass cancels the pending preview. Neither a short nor a prolonged hover opens the central panel. Keyboard focus also previews without activating. A click or keyboard activation opens the complete Nook panel with the shared spring. Pointer departure waits 280 ms before always returning to the thin discreet tab; an open panel first completes its 150 ms content exit. Reentering during that exit cancels closure and continues from the current presentation.

The native polling worker temporarily adopts the window's DPI awareness context for cursor conversion and region testing, then restores its original context on every exit path. This keeps physical coordinates consistent when Windows uses mixed scaling contexts; see Microsoft's [mixed-mode DPI guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/hidpi/high-dpi-improvements-for-desktop-applications).

Reduced motion replaces spatial animation and blur with a 150 ms fade. Hidden pages settle pending transitions without leaving stale invisible or interactive layers. Actual keyboard editing, file dialogs, drags and transfer consent retain their protection. Settings or Scenarios alone and an input left focused after a mouse click do not pin the panel. Native cursor presence takes priority over stale DOM entry; a geometry acknowledgement without mouse movement cannot wake a closed preview. An outside acknowledgement also closes an unguarded preview when the cached pointer presence was already outside, without restarting an existing closure timer. Closing cancels pending hover and keyboard intent.

Manual play, pause, resume, start and stop commands never create a notification or replace the activity preview. Track changes, task completion, automatic Pomodoro phase changes and completed file operations keep their meaningful feedback. Task completion emits one event only when a task changes from incomplete to complete. It preserves the actual task title and leaves the running timer intact. A notification may temporarily reveal its 72 px preview for 2200 ms, protected from stationary outside acknowledgements during that interval; it then returns to the thin tab. It never opens the central panel automatically. Session completion and task completion use distinct wording. A completed LocalSend band is tied to its own transfer ID and receipt/send feedback, then expires with that notification. Later track changes, unrelated file confirmations and a different transfer cannot revive the old completion. Clearing feedback releases any pending pointer-departure collapse without changing received files or active transfers.

## Visual language and access

Use the existing UI colour tokens: light primary text, muted secondary text, blue actions and selections, blue Focus accents, amber warnings and red errors. Pair status colour with a label or icon; do not rely on colour alone. Keep destructive actions distinct from ordinary navigation.

Scrollbars are hidden by default, but scrolling and keyboard access must remain available when content genuinely exceeds the screen. Prefer fitting normal content rather than hiding overflow. Retain focus-visible indicators, accessible labels, hit areas and disabled states while changing icons or spacing.

Welcome uses an idle → logo → welcome sequence: 120 ms of quiet, then a 300 × 88 px introduction and the 380 × 232 px welcome at 1.9 s. The exact V1 white shell, two round eyes and mouth remain in a 64 × 64 viewBox displayed at 56 px; no body, ears, computer or halo is added. Mimo starts centred, wakes at 420–640 ms, looks 2 px toward the name with a 3 degree tilt at 680–900 ms, and reveals the wordmark while settling into its place at 900–1260 ms. A small nod and a return to face at 1300–1700 ms precede the welcome. Every gesture lasts 220–420 ms, uses transform and opacity, and stays inside the surface. Separate SVG groups keep gaze and waking independent from the 6.8 s blink loop. Reduced motion shows the usable welcome directly. Hiding, disposal, closure or disabling motion cancels pending narrative callbacks and resets the pose; changing language does not replay the sequence. Deferred focus uses preventScroll. The three guide pages retain reversible 360 ms transitions from their current rendered values. Démarrer, guide completion and Escape all return to the actual 128 × 10 px discreet tab; there is no intermediate ready state or legacy media layer. Pomodoro dragging retains the native range control without forcing focus; keyboard focus surrounds only the duration value.

French and English are selected from the welcome screen or Settings and saved locally. Translate interface labels through [i18n.js](../i18n.js); keep user-entered tasks, file names, artist and track names intact. A language change must preserve the active session, task list and current view.

## Check a design change

Inspect the affected view at more than one display scale, with long text and sample empty/error states. Check keyboard navigation, reduced motion, interrupted transitions and the path back to a smaller view. Use before/after screenshots with sample data. Automated sizing and interaction tests are in [tests/](../tests/); they complement checks of the actual Windows contour and pointer behaviour.

## Health, modules and sharing reference update

`experience.js/css` owns the six-status Health screen and the ordered module list with a live layout preview. Unknown firewall state is explicitly unknown; a running listener does not prove that a firewall allows incoming devices. Only the first three enabled modules are visible. Disabled modules keep their stored order, additional enabled modules remain in reserve, Apply saves, and Cancel leaves the prior settings intact.

`sharing-experience.js/css` reuses the existing Shelf and LocalSend models. File actions depend on actual supported capabilities, selection is shared, favorites use certificate-derived stable identities and offline favorites cannot receive a send. Manual connection accepts private/link-local numeric IPv4 with an optional port; the Rust backend remains the authority and verifies the LocalSend TLS identity. No internet, server or account is required.

`focus-experience.css` arranges the shared timer/task model. A completed work phase records a persisted summary; its three choices use configured short-break, long-break and work durations. The timer auto-advances while the nonblocking summary is visible. Choosing a phase retargets that phase without granting another cycle. Skipped work earns no elapsed-work credit. The task prompt remains pending until the summary is dismissed.

The twelve micro-interaction references share 120 ms press, 200 ms color, 300 ms content, 500 ms spring, 80 ms entrance and 60 ms row stagger. Newly inserted rows animate once; unchanged polling does not detach them. Copy confirmation waits for the clipboard result and resets after 1600 ms. Small confirmations morph to 220 × 38 and dismiss after 2200 ms. Errors retain their message after the 450 ms shake. Transfer and clock/media progress use measured values with the shared 250 ms linear interpolation. Reduced motion removes spatial travel, blur, shake and shimmer, retaining short fades.

## Information architecture and native ownership

The header exposes only Nook, Tray and Settings. Settings contains General, Modules, Scenarios and Health; activity cards open existing detail routes. The settings shell retains one bounded content area and scrolls longer panels internally. Native settings, local diagnostics and discovery are implemented in Rust and reached through guarded Tauri commands. Existing timer deadlines, artwork analysis and user data are retained.

Shelf downloads stream through the native core into the Windows Downloads folder. The frontend supplies only opaque item IDs; it never supplies an output path. Name collisions create another copy, existing files are not overwritten, received-file origin metadata is verified and the Shelf keeps its originals. Errors remain beside the files rather than appearing as LocalSend errors.
